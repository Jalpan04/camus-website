WHO WAS CAMUS?

French-Algerian writer born 1913, died 1960 in car crash.

Nobel Prize winner 1957 for literature.

Philosopher of absurdism - life has no inherent meaning.

Journalist, playwright, novelist - fought against injustice.

MAJOR WORKS

The Stranger (1942) - Meursault kills man, feels nothing.

"Mother died today. Or maybe yesterday; I can't be sure."

The Plague (1947) - Disease isolates city, humans endure.

"There are more things to admire in men than to despise."

The Myth of Sisyphus (1942) - Suicide vs. absurd life.

"The struggle itself toward the heights is enough to fill a man's heart."

The Rebel (1951) - Revolution without murder.

"Man is the only creature who refuses to be what he is."

The Fall (1956) - Confession of failed judge.

"Don't wait for the Last Judgment. It takes place every day."

THE ABSURD

Life is meaningless - universe doesn't care about humans.

"There is but one truly serious philosophical problem, and that is suicide."

We still seek meaning - this contradiction = absurd.

"The absurd is the essential concept and the first truth."

Don't commit suicide - embrace the absurd instead.

"Living is keeping the absurd alive."

Create your own values - live fully despite meaninglessness.

"The only way to deal with an unfree world is to become so absolutely free that your very existence is an act of rebellion."

Sisyphus must be happy - find joy in the struggle.

"One must imagine Sisyphus happy."

CAMUS DIED JANUARY 4, 1960
