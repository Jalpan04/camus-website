WHO WAS CAMUS?

French-Algerian writer born 1913, died 1960 in car crash.

Nobel Prize winner 1957 for literature.

Philosopher of absurdism - life has no inherent meaning.

Journalist, playwright, novelist - fought against injustice.

MAJOR WORKS

The Stranger (1942) - Meursault kills man, feels nothing.

"Mother died today. Or maybe yesterday; I can't be sure."

The Myth of Sisyphus (1942) - Suicide vs. absurd life.

"The struggle itself toward the heights is enough to fill a man's heart."

Other Futile Works:

  • The Plague (1947)

INTERACTIVE ABSURDITY

THE ABSURD

Life is meaningless - universe doesn't care about humans.

We still seek meaning - this contradiction = absurd.

"The only way to deal with an unfree world is to become so absolutely free that your very existence is an act of rebellion."

CAMUS DIED JANUARY 4, 1960. THE ABSURD LIVES ON.
